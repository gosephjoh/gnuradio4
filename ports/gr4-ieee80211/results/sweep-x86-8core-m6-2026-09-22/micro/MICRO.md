### What the ratio is

`process_stream_to_message_ratio` is a setting of GR4's scheduler (`core/include/gnuradio-4.0/Scheduler.hpp`,
default 16). Every worker runs a loop of *passes*; in a pass it selects blocks and calls their `work()`. The
worker keeps a pass counter, and once every `ratio` passes it opens a *message phase* before doing any work.
The message phase is where everything that is not stream processing happens, in this order: the scheduler's and
graph's own messages (worker 0 only), cleanup of removed blocks, zombie reaping, adoption of new blocks, the
re-sync of every block's scheduling state (`stateSync`), the static re-ordering of the worker's block list,
each block's `processScheduledMessages()` (settings changes and other block messages), and buffer house-keeping
unless the house-keeping policy is Light. Worker 0 also opens the phase early when a scheduler message is
pending, so the ratio does not bound the latency of scheduler and graph messages; it does bound everything
else on that list. The ratio is a count of passes, not a time: the interval between two message phases is
`ratio` × the worker's pass duration, and the pass duration depends on the policy and the load (the table
below gives both for this run). A power of two is slightly cheaper (the counter wraps with a mask instead of
a modulo).

The re-sync is what makes the ratio matter to EDF. `syncSchedStates()` assigns whole scheduling states, and
under a policy that tracks releases the worker then rebuilds its release storage and discards every
outstanding job — on every message phase, whether or not the graph changed (the source records this as a
known defect). Under RR and RM the re-sync is a few microseconds; under EDF it is the storage rebuild. At the
default ratio of 16 that rebuild recurs every 16 passes, which is the `stateSync` row above: its busy share is
the share of the worker's life spent rebuilding state that did not change. At 4096 the same work recurs 256×
less often and its busy share falls below one percent, which is why the sweeps compare policies at 4096.

### Choosing and setting a value

The ratio trades scheduler overhead against reaction time. A larger ratio makes every message phase rarer:
less re-sync cost, more passes per second, but a longer wait before a settings change is applied, a new block
is adopted or a removed one is released. A smaller ratio does the reverse. The rule is: take the largest
ratio whose message-phase interval is still inside the latency the application can tolerate for a settings
change or a graph mutation, and check that `stateSync`'s busy share is negligible at that ratio. For a
static graph with no settings traffic while running, which is what the sweeps use, 4096 is that value on this
machine (an interval in the tens of milliseconds, re-sync below 1 %). A graph that expects settings messages
at a given rate needs an interval shorter than their spacing; divide that by the measured pass duration to
get the ratio, and round to a power of two. Do not pick the ratio to make one policy look better: it changes
the re-sync cost of EDF and the pass rate of every policy at once, so policies are compared at the same
ratio.

Setting it: `rx_latency4 --sched-ratio N` stages it as the scheduler setting `process_stream_to_message_ratio`
in the property map applied before the scheduler starts (staging then applying; `set()` alone changes
nothing); the sweep driver takes `sched_ratio` from the machine profile (`experiments/profiles/*.json`,
`x86-8core-r4096.json` is the 4096 variant) and passes it the same way; in GR4 code it is the constructor
setting `{"process_stream_to_message_ratio", gr::Size_t(N)}` of any `gr::scheduler::Simple`. The value each
run used is recorded as `sched_ratio` in its `latency_summary.json` and `trace_meta.json`.
